Two-week review: the system asks for your attention in the wrong shape

2026-08-05. Sources: this chat's transcript (133 owner turns, timestamped), merge history of all four repos, the open pull-request queue, and the night-shift approval record. Owner feedback prompting it: "the system is not effective, I often get confused on status, I can't recall what is the next step in each chat… I feel like a lot of things go missed."

The diagnosis, in one sentence

Every part of the system asks for your decision at its own cadence and in its own place, while your attention arrives in two daily batches at one place at a time — so decisions expire unseen, queues grow silently, and "status" is the union of four chats, four dashboards, ten pull requests and a Routine schedule nobody can hold in their head.

The per-chat options menus (a house rule) are locally right and globally useless: correct at the moment they're written, buried by the next Routine firing, invisible from the other three chats.

The fix — four pieces, smallest first

1. One Owner Inbox — the whole fleet on one page, decisions first. Generated from all four repos + the GitHub API; regenerated by the existing morning Routine and pushed to you at 6:25 am ET, before your window:

DECIDE (12 min)   · merge medtech #6 — gate is lying, 6 days   [link]
                  · merge/close SpecBuildr #49 — parked 9 days [link]
                  · …every open PR, one line, a recommendation
DO (ranked, all repos)
                  1. send LinkedIn post (W31 measure at 0, 2d left)
                  2. write NewCo goals.json (28 tasks gated)  …
EACH CHAT'S NEXT STEP
                  SpecBuildr: waiting on you to send post
                  medtech:    blocked on #6 merge, then end goal
WHAT RAN WITHOUT YOU  · NewCo captured history · night shift: nothing (no slate approved)

fleet_state.py already computes most of this. The missing input is a one-line NEXT.md each chat maintains at wrap — "waiting on / next step" — which is what kills "I can't recall what's next in each chat."

2. Flip the night shift: run-then-review instead of ask-then-run. The nightly approval gate assumed you're present every evening; you're present about two evenings a week per chat, and 5 of 7 slates died waiting. Replace the per-night menu with a standing weekly policy you approve once — e.g. "agent-owned, goal-serving or lights-on, verifiable, no credentials, no engine surgery" — and let the night shift run under it unattended. The real gate was never the evening menu; it is the draft pull request, and that stays. Cost of a bad night is still a closed pull request, never a broken main.

3. Sweep-to-zero on drafts, weekly, inside one window. Any draft older than 7 days gets merged, closed, or explicitly parked with a reason — no fourth state. The inbox opens with this list. Ten drafts is not a queue, it is a second task system with no owner.

4. Move goal scoring off your plate. A goal's number should never sit stale for 8 days: the Sunday fleet audit drafts the score with evidence (it already reads the measures), and you confirm or correct in the inbox. Setting goals stays yours alone; scoring them becomes a one-tap review.

What this does not change

The merge gate, draft-only pull requests, agents-never-publish, owner-only goals files, claim-before-you-build — untouched. This redesign moves where and when decisions reach you, not who holds them.
