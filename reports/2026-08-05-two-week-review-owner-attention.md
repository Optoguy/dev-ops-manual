# Two-week review: the system asks for your attention in the wrong shape

2026-08-05. Sources: this chat's transcript (133 owner turns, timestamped),
merge history of all four repos, the open pull-request queue, and the night-shift
approval record. Owner feedback prompting it: *"the system is not effective, I
often get confused on status, I can't recall what is the next step in each chat…
I feel like a lot of things go missed."*

The feedback is correct, and the data says why.

## Your actual availability

Two short windows, almost every working day, ET:

| Window | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **~6:30–9:00 am** | 68 of 133 chat turns land in hours 6–8; merge bursts sit here too — four SpecBuildr merges in 27 minutes on 7/31 (8:39–9:06 am), this morning's #14 at 7:24 am |
| **~7:30–9:30 pm** | 34 turns in hours 19–21; last night's #13 at 9:42 pm; NewCoEndotest bench commits cluster 7–9:30 pm |

**Attention is serial — one project owns a window.** Week one (7/22–7/30) the
window went to building the method and SpecBuildr: 110 chat turns, merges every
day. Week two it went to the NewCoEndotest bench: overnight sessions on 8/1, 8/2,
8/4 — and this chat saw a **108-hour gap** (7/31 9:00 am → 8/4 9:09 pm) while
medtech went untouched for six days. You were never away; the *other chats'*
queues just kept growing as if you were.

## What the system did with that pattern

- **Night shift:** 7 slates proposed since 7/28, **2 approved, 5 expired
  unanswered** — including one firing that re-swept 55 times and burned the
  weekly usage limit. Each unanswered night still paid the full sweep cost.
- **Pull requests:** 10 open drafts tonight; oldest 9 days. Adoption pull
  requests averaged 5–6 days to merge. Merges happen only in your two windows,
  in bursts — the queue drains at ~4 per window when you're in the mood.
- **Your task list:** ~22 owner tasks scattered across four `plan.json` files
  (16 in SpecBuildr alone), plus 10 pull requests, plus in-chat questions that
  scroll away. **No single ranked list of "what needs Dan" exists anywhere.**
- **Missed in plain sight, this fortnight:** medtech's gate lying for 6 days
  behind an unmerged fix; dev-ops-manual's weekly goal *achieved* but unscored
  for 8 days; `ai-directories` — a P1 on the week's only goal — untouched while
  the week expired; the prepared LinkedIn post unsent for 3 days with the weekly
  measure at 0.

## The diagnosis, in one sentence

**Every part of the system asks for your decision at its own cadence and in its
own place, while your attention arrives in two daily batches at one place at a
time** — so decisions expire unseen, queues grow silently, and "status" is the
union of four chats, four dashboards, ten pull requests and a Routine schedule
nobody can hold in their head.

The per-chat options menus (a house rule) are locally right and globally
useless: correct at the moment they're written, buried by the next Routine
firing, invisible from the other three chats.

## The fix — four pieces, smallest first

**1. One Owner Inbox — the whole fleet on one page, decisions first.**
Generated from all four repos + the GitHub API; regenerated by the existing
morning Routine and pushed to you at 6:25 am ET, before your window:

```
DECIDE (12 min)   · merge medtech #6 — gate is lying, 6 days   [link]
                  · merge/close SpecBuildr #49 — parked 9 days [link]
                  · …every open PR, one line, a recommendation
DO (ranked, all repos)
                  1. send LinkedIn post (W31 measure at 0, 2d left)
                  2. write NewCo goals.json (28 tasks gated)  …
EACH CHAT'S NEXT STEP
                  SpecBuildr: waiting on you to send post
                  medtech:    blocked on #6 merge, then end goal
WHAT RAN WITHOUT YOU  · NewCo captured history · night shift: nothing (no slate approved)
```

`fleet_state.py` already computes most of this. The missing input is a one-line
`NEXT.md` each chat maintains at wrap — "waiting on / next step" — which is what
kills "I can't recall what's next in each chat."

**2. Flip the night shift: run-then-review instead of ask-then-run.**
The nightly approval gate assumed you're present every evening; you're present
about two evenings a week per chat, and 5 of 7 slates died waiting. Replace the
per-night menu with a **standing weekly policy** you approve once — e.g.
"agent-owned, goal-serving or lights-on, verifiable, no credentials, no engine
surgery" — and let the night shift run under it unattended. **The real gate was
never the evening menu; it is the draft pull request**, and that stays. Cost of a
bad night is still a closed pull request, never a broken main.

**3. Sweep-to-zero on drafts, weekly, inside one window.**
Any draft older than 7 days gets merged, closed, or explicitly parked with a
reason — no fourth state. The inbox opens with this list. Ten drafts is not a
queue, it is a second task system with no owner.

**4. Move goal scoring off your plate.**
A goal's number should never sit stale for 8 days: the Sunday fleet audit drafts
the score with evidence (it already reads the measures), and you confirm or
correct in the inbox. Setting goals stays yours alone; *scoring* them becomes a
one-tap review.

## What this does not change

The merge gate, draft-only pull requests, agents-never-publish, owner-only goals
files, claim-before-you-build — untouched. This redesign moves **where and when
decisions reach you**, not who holds them.
