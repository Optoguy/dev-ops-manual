# Make the deliverable feel like a prize

The work is the process; the deliverable is the reward — and research says the
reward moment is disproportionately what users remember and share. The most
common failure is a product that nails the work and then hands over a flat
printout. **Design the last moment first.**

## The psychology (the four levers)

- **Peak-end rule (Kahneman).** People judge an experience by its peak and its
  ending, not its average. Identify your peak (the "aha", the caught mistake, the
  hard number) and make the ending land — the last 30 seconds carry the memory.
- **IKEA effect.** People overvalue what they helped build. The deliverable must
  *visibly contain the user's own inputs* — their words, their numbers, their
  trade-offs — so it reads as "mine," not "a template anyone would get."
- **Personalization.** A named, dated, serial-numbered artifact reads as a real
  object; an anonymous one reads as output. "Prepared for <name> · <their project
  in their words> · <serial>" costs nothing and changes the object's nature.
- **Shareability is the growth loop.** People share what makes them look smart. A
  seal, a show-your-work appendix, a clean cover — screenshot bait — and every
  share is an acquisition channel. Design *for the screenshot*.

## Concrete moves

1. **A cover that is theirs.** A title derived from their own intent, the
   signature visual drawn large *with their numbers on it*, a serial, a date, a
   prepared-for line. The cover is the screenshot.
2. **A seal / verdict.** A stamped result only your product can produce ("checked
   · 3 constraints tested · 1 flag negotiated"). It's both brand and proof of
   rigor.
3. **Show your work.** An appendix with each derived number as a worked step
   (`3000 Wh ÷ (5.5 h × 0.75) = 730 W`). Experts verify it, novices learn from it,
   both trust it — and trust gets shared.
4. **A real ending on screen.** Two seconds of in-world ceremony before the
   download (the artifact assembles, the seal thunks on), **skippable and
   reduced-motion-safe.**
5. **The last line is human.** End with one warm, in-character line
   ("Built from your answers in 9 minutes — go build the real thing."). The end
   is what they remember.

## Anti-goals

- **Decoration without information.** Every flourish must carry data — the seal
  carries the check count, the serial carries the type + date. Ornament that says
  nothing is noise.
- **Choice instead of an opinion.** Ten competent style options produce zero wow.
  **One opinionated deliverable, perfected, beats a picker.** (Hard-won: a
  ten-style exploration yielded ten competent looks and no delight.)
- **Novelty that breaks utility.** The functional core stays ruthlessly clean and
  usable; wow lives on the cover, the seal, and the appendix — never at the cost
  of the part someone actually has to *use*.

## Judgment

- **Find the real peak before decorating.** If the product has no genuine peak
  (no caught error, no earned number, no insight), no amount of ceremony will
  rescue the ending — build the peak first, then frame it.
- **The second reader matters.** Often the deliverable's real customer is whoever
  the user hands it to next. Serve them too (checkboxes at the workbench, a clean
  quotable table) — that reader is the referral.
